Mission & History

The Institute for Advanced Intelligence Study (IAIS) studies natural intelligence through computation and translates its principles into artificial intelligence.

Probe Intelligence to Advance Intelligence.

Our Mission

The history of artificial intelligence has, in effect, been a history of psychology. Each advance in our understanding of behaviour, cognition and the brain has reshaped how machines learn. IAIS continues this tradition by design.

Intelligence is not a matter of scale. It is a matter of mechanism. We take what the organism does, implement it as an algorithm, and then generalise its form until it becomes a principle that machines can share. A cognitive process that is understood precisely enough to be written as a general algorithm is, in itself, artificial intelligence.

Our Agenda: Intelligence of, by and for the Human

Our research addresses the human brain, mind, behaviour and society, and extends into the domains that govern human life most directly: law, medicine and pharmacology. Our methods are computational and formal, analytical, and human-centred. The agenda has three parts.

  • AI by the human: We build artificial agents from the computational mechanisms of the brain, from prefrontal meta-control to causal inference and social norms. Center for Computational Brain & AI →
  • AI for the human: We put that intelligence to work for people. AI that understands how a person thinks and learns can optimise the test that screens them and the treatment that helps them, which is the scientific basis of digital therapeutics. Center for Medicine, Pharmacology & AI →
  • AI of the human: Every AI system has learned something that we do not fully understand. Value alignment is a psychological problem before it is an engineering one, so we ask what AI knows, what it does and what it means, through the lens of psychology. Center for AI Psychology & Digital Physiology →

Intelligence also lives in society. Legal and normative judgment shows both the reach and the limit of computation: its process can be formalised, while its normative purpose remains a human responsibility. Center for Computational Law →

How We Work

IAIS draws on three established institutional models.

  • Academic model (Santa Fe Institute): Research at IAIS is driven by questions, not by disciplines. The institute runs on a fully project-based system. Researchers define the problem, write the proposal, conduct the study and share the results.
  • Governance model (Igalia): IAIS is owned by its researchers. The institute is incorporated as a company, yet every share belongs to the researchers who work here. We take no equity investment, so no shareholder can redirect our scientific agenda.
  • Operational model (Fraunhofer): IAIS works with partners who bring real problems and the funding to address them. We welcome industry, public bodies and individuals alike, and we transfer our findings to the partners who need them. Research that serves the public good or pure curiosity is equally welcome.

Why It Matters

Few institutes in Korea operate in this way. Government-driven research follows policy cycles, which change. Fundamental questions do not. IAIS exists to pursue those questions with independence, because deep understanding is what solves real problems in the end.

Our History

IAIS was founded on 3 December 2024 by a cognitive scientist, an AI scientist, a law professor, a psychologist and a medical doctor. Our work has since appeared in leading JCR-ranked journals and at flagship venues in decision-making, neuroscience and computer science.

Looking Ahead

We will work with leading universities and institutes worldwide and with researchers across Korea, without regard to disciplinary lines. We will hold our research to the highest international standard. We will extend the frontiers of knowledge, often in places where few are looking. Our aim is to move from Natural General Intelligence to Artificial General yet Verified Intelligence.
